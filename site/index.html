How it works

Every bet on the board goes through the same pipeline:

  1. Team ratings. Each team gets an offense and a defense rating, fit on every game over the last season or so. Recent games count more, and opponents' strength is built in, so beating good teams counts more than beating bad ones.
  2. Game adjustments. The model accounts for home field, rest and back-to-backs. In MLB it also uses the starting pitchers (FIP, recent starts) and park factors. For NFL and NHL it checks key injuries like a starting QB or goalie.
  3. Score simulation. Ratings become a full distribution of possible scores, built to fit each sport: NFL margins cluster at 3 and 7, NHL has empty-net goals, MLB has walk-offs, and soccer uses a low-scoring draw model. That distribution prices every moneyline, spread and total.
  4. Market check. DraftKings prices get the bookmaker's cut (the vig) removed and are blended with our model. The market is very sharp, so a pick shows an edge only when the data genuinely disagrees with it.
  5. Calibration. A walk-forward backtest replays past seasons without peeking at the future and tunes each sport so a "60%" pick hits about 60% of the time.

Grades: A means clearly +EV (our probability is at least 2% above the price's break-even) and more likely than not. B means +EV. C means close to a fair price. D means the price is too steep for what the data supports.

No model hits 100%. Sportsbooks build a margin into every price, and parlays multiply it. A 3-leg parlay of 70% legs still loses about two times in three. Treat this as a research tool, bet only what you can afford to lose, and if gambling stops being fun, call 1-800-GAMBLER.
